AthenaX Launchpad — Project Selection Criteria
1. Purpose
The Launchpad currently targets a 70/30 mix: roughly 70% new and early-stage projects (pre-seed through Series A, actively fundraising or recently launched) and 30% established, high-profile projects that anchor credibility and attract traffic. 


2. Target Sectors
All projects must fall within one of AthenaX's seven sectors:


Sector
	Scope
	AI & Agents
	Foundation models, fine-tuning platforms, autonomous agents, AI infrastructure, MLOps, AI-native applications
	Biotech
	Drug discovery, synthetic biology, genomics, longevity, diagnostics, lab automation, computational biology
	Crypto
	L1/L2 protocols, DEXs, CEXs, wallets, ZK-proofs, bridges, DePIN, stablecoins, on-chain credit, DAOs
	Developer Tools
	SDKs, APIs, code editors, CI/CD, testing, monitoring, database tooling, developer platforms
	Infrastructure
	Cloud, compute, networking, storage, security, edge, data pipelines, orchestration
	Robotics
	Autonomous vehicles, drones, industrial robots, humanoids, manipulation hardware, ROS tooling, sim-to-real
	RWA
	Tokenized real estate, commodities, treasuries, private credit, carbon credits, trade finance, asset-backed lending, fractionalization platforms, compliance and oracle infrastructure for off-chain asset verification
	

3. New Project Criteria (70% of Pipeline)
A new project is pre-product-market-fit, early in its go-to-market, or actively fundraising. These are the companies that most need discovery and that AthenaX exists to surface.
3.1 Minimum Requirements 
Criterion
	Threshold
	How to verify
	Working product
	Functional prototype, beta, or live product. Not just a whitepaper or landing page with an email capture.
	Visit the product URL, check for a demo, or verify GitHub activity.
	Website
	Live, functional website with clear product description.
	Manual check or HTTP status + content crawl.
	GitHub presence (optional, if X is there)

	Twitter / X presence (optional, if GitHub is there)
	Active account. Minimum ~1,000 followers acceptable for very early projects; ~3,000+ preferred.
	X API: follower count, posting frequency, engagement rate.
	Team identifiability
	At least one founder or core team member is publicly identifiable (real name, LinkedIn, or prior verifiable work).
	Manual check.
	Sector fit
	Falls clearly within one of the seven sectors.
	Manual classification or AI-assisted tagging.

	3.3 Disqualifiers (immediate rejection)
* No working product (vaporware, concept-only, "coming soon" with no evidence of build)
* Obvious scam indicators (copied whitepaper, fake team photos, plagiarized code)
* Token-only projects with no underlying product or protocol utility
* Projects that have been inactive for 3+ months (no commits, no posts, no updates)
* Projects already widely known and covered (these belong in the 30% established category instead)


4. Established Project Criteria (30% of Pipeline)
Established projects serve as trust anchors. When a visitor sees recognizable names alongside unknown ones, the unknown ones gain credibility by association. 
4.1 Selection Criteria
Criterion
	Threshold
	How to verify
	Market presence
	Listed on CoinMarketCap and/or CoinGecko (for crypto). For non-crypto: recognized in major industry reports, press, or analyst coverage.
	CMC/CoinGecko API, Crunchbase, press index.
	Tier status
	Tier 1 or Tier 2 within their sector. Tier 1 = household name in the industry. Tier 2 = well-known among practitioners, top 20 in their niche.
	Market cap ranking, user base, developer adoption metrics, and industry reports.
	Sector leadership
	Demonstrably a leader or major player in at least one sub-vertical (e.g., leading L2, top DEX by volume, dominant CI/CD tool).
	Market share data, TVL rankings, usage statistics, developer surveys.
	Active development
	Still actively shipping, not legacy or stagnant.
	GitHub activity, product changelog, recent announcements.
	Brand fit
	Represents the kind of "frontier tech" AthenaX is associated with. Consumer social apps, lifestyle brands, and low-tech SaaS don't fit.
	Editorial judgment.
	4.2 Sector-Specific Anchors to Target
Cryptoi: L1/L2 leaders (Ethereum, Solana, Arbitrum, Optimism, Base), top DEXs (Uniswap, Jupiter), major wallets (MetaMask, Phantom, Rabby), ZK pioneers (zkSync, Starknet, Aztec), established projectsi (Aave, Lido, EigenLayer).


AI & Agents: Foundation model companies (Anthropic, Mistral, Cohere), agent frameworks (LangChain, CrewAI), AI-native tools with significant adoption, MLOps leaders.


Developer Tools: Category leaders in their niche — the tools developers actually use and talk about on Hacker News.


Infrastructure: Cloud-native leaders, major open-source infrastructure projects, compute and networking innovators.


Biotech: Well-funded biotech startups with published research and clinical/regulatory milestones. Companies featured in Nature, Science, or STAT News.


Robotics: Companies with demonstrated hardware, real-world deployments, or significant venture backing from robotics-focused funds.

RWA: Tokenization leaders (Ondo Finance, Centrifuge, Maple Finance), infrastructure enabling RWA rails (Chainlink CCIP, Goldfinch), institutional-grade platforms (Securitize, Backed Finance), and projects bridging TradFi assets on-chain (treasury tokenization, private credit protocols, real estate fractionalization). Focus on projects with real AUM or TVL, not just tokenization concepts.

5. Additional Refinements — Recommendations
5.1 "Velocity" Signal
Instead of just looking at absolute numbers (follower count, star count), the model should heavily weight growth rate. A project that went from 200 to 2,000 GitHub stars in 30 days is a stronger signal than one that's had 5,000 stars for two years. Same for Twitter followers, commit frequency, and community size. 
5.2 VC Portfolio Monitoring — Expanded List


* Generalist Tier 1: Sequoia, Founders Fund, Index Ventures, Accel, Lightspeed
* Crypto-focused: Paradigm, Polychain, Pantera, Multicoin, Framework Ventures, Dragonfly
* AI-focused: Coatue, Tiger Global (AI bets), Radical Ventures, Conviction Partners
* Deep tech / Bio: Lux Capital, a16z Bio, Flagship Pioneering, GV, DCVC
* Robotics: Khosla Ventures, Eclipse Ventures, Toyota Ventures
* Developer tools: Heavybit, Redpoint, Unusual Ventures, Greylock
* RWA / TradFi-crypto crossover: ParaFi Capital, Superstate, Galaxy Digital, Brevan Howard Digital, Hamilton Lane (for institutional tokenization bets)


When any of these funds announces a new investment in a relevant sector, the model should immediately flag the portfolio company for evaluation.
5.3 Conference & Event Pipeline
Major conferences are concentrated discovery moments. The model should monitor speaker lists, exhibitor directories, and hackathon winners from events like:


* AI: NeurIPS, ICML, demo days at AI Grant, Cerebral Valley events
* Crypto: ETHDenver, Devconnect, Consensus, Token2049, hackathon winners (ETHGlobal)
* Biotech: JP Morgan Healthcare Conference, BIO, SXSW Health
* Robotics: IROS, CoRL, RoboCup, CES robotics pavilion
* Dev Tools: KubeCon, GitHub Universe, DevRelCon, infrastructure-focused YC Demo Days
